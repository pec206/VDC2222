Root Canal Retreatment in Abu Dhabi

Previous root canal not healing? Retreatment removes the old filling material, eliminates persistent infection, and saves the tooth. All endodontic cases reviewed by Medical Director Dr. Hussien Tahoun.

When Does a Root Canal Need Retreatment?

A root canal treated tooth can last for decades, but sometimes the initial treatment does not fully resolve the infection or the tooth develops new problems. Retreatment becomes necessary when the original root canal fails to heal or when new issues compromise the tooth from within.

Common reasons a previously treated tooth may need retreatment include:

  • Incomplete initial treatment — missed canals, unfilled canal segments, or inadequate cleaning left bacteria behind
  • New decay under the crown — a leaking or broken restoration allowed bacteria to re-enter the canal system
  • Broken instrument — a separated file fragment from the original procedure that now harbours infection
  • Persistent or recurring infection — bacteria survived the first treatment due to complex root anatomy
  • Delayed restoration — the tooth was not crowned promptly after the original root canal, allowing contamination
  • New fracture or crack — a crack in the root or crown exposes the filled canal to oral bacteria

Signs You May Need Retreatment

If you have had a root canal and notice any of the following symptoms, the tooth may require retreatment:

  • Recurring pain — spontaneous or biting pain in a previously treated tooth
  • Swelling — gum swelling near the treated tooth, possibly with pus discharge
  • Sinus tract or fistula — a small pimple-like bump on the gum that drains intermittently
  • Sensitivity to heat — lingering discomfort when drinking hot beverages
  • Darkening tooth — the treated tooth is changing colour, suggesting internal breakdown
  • X-ray findings — periapical radiolucency (a dark area at the root tip) visible on an OPG panoramic X-ray taken during consultation

Important Distinction

Not every symptom after a root canal means retreatment is needed. Post-operative sensitivity for the first few weeks is normal. Retreatment is considered when symptoms persist or return months to years after the original treatment, or when imaging reveals a new or growing periapical lesion.

The Retreatment Process

Endodontic retreatment follows a systematic protocol to remove the old filling, eliminate infection, and reseal the canals. At Vision Dental, the process typically takes 2–3 appointments.

Diagnosis & Imaging

Clinical examination and an OPG panoramic X-ray (used during consultation) to assess the extent of infection, identify missed canals, and evaluate root anatomy. Dr. Hussien Tahoun reviews the imaging and confirms the retreatment plan.

Crown & Post Removal

The existing crown or restoration is carefully removed to access the root canal system. If a post is cemented inside the canal, it is removed using ultrasonic vibration to avoid damaging the root.

Old Filling Material Removal

The previous gutta-percha and sealer are removed from the canals using heat-treated rotary files. Solvents may be used to soften older filling materials. Every canal is cleared down to its full working length.

Re-Cleaning & Shaping

The canals are re-instrumented with the endodontic motor and NiTi rotary files. An electronic apex locator confirms the precise canal length. Copious irrigation with sodium hypochlorite and EDTA eliminates bacteria and dissolves debris. Chairside X-ray is used during treatment to verify canal preparation.

Medicated Dressing (If Needed)

In cases of severe infection, calcium hydroxide medication is placed inside the canals and sealed with a temporary filling. This intra-canal dressing kills residual bacteria over 1–2 weeks before final obturation.

Re-Obturation

Once the canals are clean and symptoms have resolved, the canals are dried and filled with new gutta-percha and bioceramic sealer, creating a hermetic seal that prevents reinfection.

New Restoration

The tooth is rebuilt with a core and protected with a new dental crown. A properly fitted crown is essential to prevent bacterial leakage and protect the weakened tooth structure.

Retreatment vs Extraction

The decision between retreatment and extraction depends on several clinical factors. Saving a natural tooth is always preferred when the prognosis is favourable.

FactorRetreatment PreferredExtraction Preferred
Root integrityNo vertical fracture, roots intactVertical root fracture confirmed
Bone supportAdequate bone around the rootSevere bone loss, poor prognosis
Tooth restorabilityEnough tooth structure for a crownTooth too broken down to restore
Root resorptionMinimal or no resorptionSevere external or internal resorption
Strategic valueKey tooth for chewing or bridge supportNon-strategic tooth, implant is viable

If extraction is the better option, Vision Dental offers tooth extraction followed by replacement with a dental implant or dental bridge. Dr. Hussien Tahoun will discuss all options during your consultation.

Technology We Use

Endodontic Motor with Apex Locator

NiTi rotary file system with an integrated electronic apex locator for precise canal length measurement and controlled instrumentation — reducing the risk of perforation and ensuring thorough cleaning.

STA Digital Anesthesia

The STA (Single Tooth Anesthesia) system delivers computer-controlled local anesthesia for a virtually painless injection — particularly important for retreatment cases where teeth can be more sensitive.

Chairside X-Ray

Used during treatment only to verify canal length, confirm complete removal of old filling material, and check the quality of the final obturation in real time.

OPG Panoramic X-Ray

Used during consultation for initial diagnosis — provides a full view of both jaws, revealing periapical lesions, missed canals, and the overall condition of the treated tooth and surrounding structures.

Common Questions

Root Canal Retreatment FAQs

Why did my root canal fail?
Root canals can fail for several reasons: a missed canal that was not cleaned, complex root anatomy that prevented complete disinfection, a cracked or leaking crown that allowed bacteria to re-enter, or new decay forming around the restoration. In some cases, a separated instrument from the original procedure may harbour persistent infection.
Is root canal retreatment painful?
Retreatment is performed under local anesthesia and should not be painful during the procedure. At Vision Dental, we use STA (Single Tooth Anesthesia) digital injection for a virtually painless experience. Some mild tenderness is normal for a few days after the appointment, manageable with over-the-counter pain relief.
How many appointments does retreatment take?
Retreatment typically requires 2–3 appointments. The first visit involves removing the old restoration and cleaning the canals. If infection is significant, medicated dressing is placed for 1–2 weeks before the final filling at the next appointment. A follow-up visit confirms healing.
What is the success rate of retreatment?
Endodontic retreatment has a success rate of approximately 75–85%, depending on the reason for failure and the tooth’s condition. Teeth with missed canals or inadequate initial filling respond particularly well. If retreatment is unsuccessful, apicoectomy (surgical root tip removal) or extraction with implant replacement may be considered.
Can I keep my crown during retreatment?
In most retreatment cases, the existing crown must be removed to gain proper access to the canals. Occasionally, a small access hole can be made through the crown, but this depends on the crown type and the canals that need retreatment. A new crown is usually recommended after retreatment for the best seal and long-term outcome.
How much does root canal retreatment cost in Abu Dhabi?
Retreatment cost varies depending on the tooth (front vs molar), the complexity of the case, and whether a post needs to be removed. Visit our root canal cost page for detailed pricing. A personalised quote is provided after your diagnostic consultation at Vision Dental.
