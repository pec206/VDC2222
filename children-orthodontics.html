Children’s Orthodontics Abu Dhabi

Early orthodontic assessment from age 7 can prevent complex problems later. Prof. Amr Labib provides interceptive treatment, braces, and Invisalign Teen for children and adolescents at Vision Dental Clinic.

When Should a Child First See an Orthodontist?

The American Association of Orthodontists and Prof. Amr Labib both recommend that every child has their first orthodontic assessment by age 7. At this age, the first permanent molars and incisors have usually erupted, giving the orthodontist enough information to detect developing problems early.

An early assessment does not mean treatment starts immediately. In many cases, no action is needed — the orthodontist simply monitors growth and development at regular intervals. However, if an issue is identified, early intervention can be simpler, shorter, and more effective than waiting until all permanent teeth have erupted.

Early Intervention — Phase 1 Treatment

Phase 1 (interceptive) orthodontics is carried out while a child still has a mix of baby and permanent teeth, typically between ages 7 and 10. The goal is to guide jaw growth, create space for erupting permanent teeth, and correct problems that would become more difficult to treat later.

Common Phase 1 interventions include:

  • Palatal expanders — widen a narrow upper jaw to correct crossbite and create space for crowded teeth
  • Space maintainers — hold the gap open when a baby tooth is lost too early, preventing neighbouring teeth from drifting into the space
  • Guiding jaw growth — functional appliances that encourage the lower jaw to grow forward in cases of a recessed chin or significant overbite
  • Correcting crossbite — early correction prevents asymmetric jaw growth and uneven wear on permanent teeth
  • Breaking harmful habits — habit-breaking appliances for prolonged thumb sucking or tongue thrusting that can distort the dental arch

Phase 1 treatment typically lasts 6–12 months, followed by a monitoring period until the remaining permanent teeth erupt.

Phase 2 Treatment — Full Orthodontics

Phase 2 begins once all (or most) permanent teeth have erupted, usually between ages 11 and 14. This is comprehensive orthodontic treatment using full braces or Invisalign Teen to align every tooth, close remaining gaps, and refine the bite.

If Phase 1 was completed successfully, Phase 2 is often shorter and less complex than it would have been without early intervention. Some children who had Phase 1 may not need Phase 2 at all — Prof. Amr Labib will advise based on progress.

Signs Your Child May Need Orthodontics

Parents should watch for the following signs that may indicate an orthodontic problem is developing:

  • Crowding or overlapping teeth — permanent teeth coming in crooked or with insufficient space
  • Crossbite — upper teeth biting inside the lower teeth on one or both sides
  • Open bite — front teeth do not meet when the back teeth are together
  • Thumb or finger sucking — habits persisting beyond age 5 can push teeth out of alignment
  • Early or late loss of baby teeth — losing teeth significantly earlier or later than expected can cause spacing or crowding issues
  • Difficulty chewing or biting — pain or discomfort when eating may indicate bite misalignment
  • Mouth breathing — habitual mouth breathing can affect jaw development and facial growth
  • Protruding front teeth — upper teeth that stick out are more vulnerable to injury

If you notice any of these signs, book a consultation so Prof. Amr Labib can evaluate your child’s development and recommend the right timing for treatment.

Treatment Options for Children

Metal Braces

The most common option for children. Durable, effective for all case complexities, and available with coloured elastic bands that kids enjoy choosing.

Ceramic Braces

Tooth-coloured brackets for older teens who want a less visible option. Same mechanics as metal braces with a more discreet appearance.

Invisalign Teen

Clear removable aligners for appropriate teen cases. Includes compliance indicators so parents and the orthodontist can verify wearing time.

Interceptive Appliances

Palatal expanders, space maintainers, and functional appliances used in Phase 1 to guide jaw growth before full braces are needed.

What Parents Should Know

Treatment Duration

Phase 1 typically lasts 6–12 months. Phase 2 (full braces or aligners) usually takes 12–24 months depending on case complexity. Prof. Amr Labib provides an estimated timeline at the start of each phase.

Adjustment Visits

Your child will need adjustment appointments every 4–6 weeks. These visits are quick — typically 15–20 minutes — and are essential for monitoring progress and making wire or appliance adjustments.

Diet with Braces

Children with braces should avoid hard foods (ice, hard sweets, popcorn kernels), sticky foods (toffee, chewing gum), and biting directly into hard items like apples or corn on the cob. Cut food into small pieces instead.

Oral Hygiene

Braces create extra surfaces where plaque can accumulate. Children must brush after every meal, use interdental brushes to clean around brackets and wires, and floss daily. We teach proper technique at each visit.

Retainers After Treatment

Once braces are removed, retainers are essential to hold teeth in their new position. We provide both fixed (bonded wire) and removable retainer options. Without retention, teeth can shift back over time.

Making Orthodontics Comfortable

Modern braces are significantly smaller and more comfortable than those used a generation ago. The brackets are low-profile, the wires are gentler, and the overall experience is much improved.

For younger children, coloured elastic bands turn braces into something fun — kids can choose their favourite colours or match them to holidays and school events. This helps children feel positive about their treatment rather than self-conscious.

Prof. Amr Labib has extensive experience working with young patients. He explains every step to both parents and children, ensures the child is comfortable throughout treatment, and makes adjustment visits as quick and comfortable as possible.

First Assessment at Age 7

Even if you do not see obvious problems, an orthodontic check at age 7 gives Prof. Amr Labib the chance to identify developing issues early. Many conditions are easier and less expensive to correct when caught before all permanent teeth have erupted. Book a free assessment — most children will simply be monitored, and treatment is recommended only when there is a clear benefit.

Common Questions

Children’s Orthodontics FAQs

At what age should my child first see an orthodontist?
The recommended age for a first orthodontic assessment is 7. By this age, enough permanent teeth have erupted for the orthodontist to detect developing problems. Early evaluation does not mean early treatment — in many cases, the orthodontist will simply monitor your child’s development and recommend treatment only when the timing is right.
What is the difference between Phase 1 and Phase 2 treatment?
Phase 1 (interceptive) treatment is done while a child still has baby teeth, typically ages 7–10. It addresses specific problems like crossbite, narrow jaws, or space loss using expanders, space maintainers, or functional appliances. Phase 2 is comprehensive treatment with full braces or Invisalign Teen once most permanent teeth have erupted, usually ages 11–14.
How long does children’s orthodontic treatment take?
Phase 1 treatment typically lasts 6–12 months. Phase 2 (full braces or aligners) usually takes 12–24 months depending on the complexity of the case. Some children who complete Phase 1 may need a shorter Phase 2 or may not need it at all. Prof. Amr Labib provides an estimated timeline after assessment.
Can my teenager get Invisalign instead of braces?
Yes, Invisalign Teen is available for appropriate cases. It works best for mild to moderate alignment issues in responsible teens who will wear the aligners 20–22 hours per day. The aligners include compliance indicators so parents and the orthodontist can verify wearing time. For complex cases or younger children, traditional braces are usually more effective.
Do braces hurt children?
Children may feel mild soreness for 2–3 days after braces are placed and after each adjustment. This is normal and manageable with over-the-counter pain relief and soft foods. Modern braces are smaller and gentler than older designs. Most children adapt quickly, and the discomfort is brief compared to the months of comfortable wear between adjustments.
