Full-Arch Implants

Replace an entire jaw of missing or failing teeth with 4–6 strategically placed Straumann® implants and a fixed, permanent bridge — designed to look and function like natural teeth.

What Are Full-Arch Implants?

Full-arch implant treatment replaces all the teeth in one jaw (upper, lower, or both) using 4–6 dental implants as anchors for a fixed bridge. Unlike removable dentures, this bridge is permanently attached — you don't take it out, and it doesn't move.

The concept is often called "All-on-4" or "All-on-6" depending on the number of implants used. The exact number is determined by your bone density, jaw anatomy, and bite requirements — not by a marketing label.

All-on-4 vs. All-on-6

All-on-4 4 implants

Uses two straight anterior implants and two tilted posterior implants to maximise bone contact without grafting.

  • Often avoids sinus lift in the upper jaw
  • Suitable when posterior bone is limited
  • Slightly less support for very long spans

All-on-6 6 implants

Adds two additional implants for greater distribution of bite forces across the arch.

  • More support for patients with strong bite
  • Better force distribution on longer bridges
  • May require bone grafting in some areas

Dr. Hussien determines the optimal number during your CBCT assessment. The goal is always the fewest implants that provide reliable long-term support for your specific anatomy.

Who Is This For?

Full-arch implants are the definitive solution for patients who:

  • Are edentulous (no remaining teeth) — currently wearing full dentures or have had all teeth extracted
  • Have failing dentition — multiple teeth with advanced decay, fractures, or periodontal disease beyond individual repair
  • Struggle with dentures — loose-fitting, uncomfortable, or causing sore spots and difficulty eating
  • Want a fixed solution — no more adhesives, no removal at night, no embarrassing slippage

The Process

Comprehensive Assessment

CBCT 3D scan, clinical examination, and bite analysis. Dr. Hussien evaluates bone volume at multiple sites, identifies nerve positions, and plans the optimal implant positions digitally.

Extractions & Implant Surgery

Remaining teeth are extracted (if any), implants are placed, and a provisional (temporary) bridge is attached — often on the same day. This "teeth-in-a-day" approach means you leave the clinic with functional teeth.

Healing Period

Over 3–6 months, the implants integrate with the bone. You wear the provisional bridge during this time — eating, speaking, and smiling normally while the foundation solidifies.

Final Prosthesis

Once fully healed, digital impressions are taken and your final bridge is designed and milled in our in-house CAD/CAM lab — using high-strength zirconia or hybrid materials for durability and aesthetics.

Full-Arch Implants vs. Dentures

  • Stability — Implant bridges are fixed. Dentures rely on suction or adhesive and can shift during eating or speaking.
  • Bone preservation — Implants stimulate the jawbone. Dentures accelerate bone loss over time, causing facial collapse.
  • Bite force — Implant-supported bridges restore near-natural bite strength. Dentures reduce bite force by 60–80%.
  • Taste and comfort — Upper implant bridges don't cover the palate, preserving taste sensation and reducing gag reflex.
  • Maintenance — Implant bridges are cleaned like natural teeth. Dentures require daily removal, soaking, and relining every few years.
  • Longevity — A well-maintained implant bridge can last 15–20+ years. Dentures typically need replacement every 5–8 years.

Bone Requirements

One of the advantages of the All-on-4 concept is that tilted posterior implants can often engage available bone without grafting. However, some patients still require:

  • Sinus lift — if upper jaw bone height is insufficient near the sinus cavities
  • Bone grafting — if severe bone loss has occurred from long-term denture wear or periodontal disease
  • Zygomatic implants — in cases of extreme upper jaw bone loss, longer implants anchored in the cheekbone may be considered

These requirements are identified during your CBCT assessment and included in your treatment plan before any decisions are made.

Common Questions

Full-Arch FAQs

Can I get teeth the same day as surgery?
In many cases, yes. A provisional bridge can be attached to the implants on the same day as surgery — this is the "teeth-in-a-day" approach. You leave the clinic with functional teeth while the implants heal underneath. The final prosthesis is placed after full integration, typically 3–6 months later.
How many implants do I need for a full jaw?
Most full-arch cases use 4–6 implants per jaw. The exact number is determined by your bone density, jaw anatomy, and bite requirements — assessed through a CBCT scan during your consultation. The goal is always the minimum number that provides reliable long-term support.
Is the surgery painful?
The procedure is performed under local anaesthesia, with sedation options available for anxious patients. Most patients report that post-operative discomfort is manageable with prescribed medication. Swelling and bruising are normal for the first week and resolve progressively.
What can I eat with full-arch implants?
During the healing phase, a soft diet is recommended. Once the final prosthesis is placed, most patients can eat a wide range of foods comfortably — including foods that are difficult or impossible with dentures. Your bite strength is significantly closer to natural teeth than what dentures provide.
How long do full-arch implants last?
The implants themselves are designed to last decades with proper maintenance. The prosthetic bridge may need refurbishment or replacement after 15–20 years depending on wear. Regular check-ups and good oral hygiene are essential for long-term success.
What if I've been told I don't have enough bone?
The All-on-4 technique was specifically developed for patients with reduced bone. Tilted posterior implants engage available bone without grafting in many cases. When bone loss is severe, options include bone grafting, sinus lifts, or zygomatic implants. A CBCT scan during your assessment determines what's possible.
