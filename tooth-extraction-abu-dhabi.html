When Is Tooth Extraction Necessary?

Tooth extraction is the removal of a tooth from its socket in the jawbone. While preserving natural teeth is always the priority, extraction becomes necessary when a tooth is too damaged or diseased to be saved. Your dentist or oral surgeon will recommend extraction only after all restorative options have been considered.

Common reasons for tooth extraction include:

  • Severe decay — when the tooth structure is too compromised for a crown, filling, or root canal to restore it
  • Advanced periodontal disease — when bone loss around the tooth has made it loose and unsupportable
  • Fractured or cracked roots — vertical root fractures that cannot be repaired and cause persistent infection
  • Failed root canal — when retreatment is not possible or has already been attempted without success
  • Orthodontic reasons — extraction of premolars to create space for alignment during orthodontic treatment
  • Supernumerary teeth — extra teeth that are causing crowding, impaction, or preventing normal eruption

Types of Extraction

There are two main types of tooth extraction, and the approach depends on the tooth’s condition, position, and root anatomy.

The Extraction Process

Assessment & OPG Panorama

Dr. Hussien evaluates the tooth clinically and takes an OPG panoramic X-ray during your consultation. The panorama shows root shape, proximity to nerves and sinuses, and surrounding bone condition — all critical for planning a safe extraction.

STA Digital Anesthesia

We use the STA (Single Tooth Anesthesia) system for computer-controlled local anesthesia delivery. The STA regulates injection speed and pressure, making the numbing process significantly more comfortable than a traditional syringe.

Extraction Technique

For simple extractions, the tooth is loosened with an elevator and removed with forceps. For surgical cases, Dr. Hussien raises a small gum flap, removes any obstructing bone, and may section the tooth into pieces for easier, less traumatic removal.

Socket Management

If you plan to replace the tooth with an implant, socket preservation with bone grafting may be performed immediately after extraction. This maintains the bone volume needed for future implant placement and prevents the ridge from collapsing.

Suturing & Post-Op Instructions

Surgical sites are closed with sutures. You receive detailed aftercare instructions covering bleeding control, diet, medication, and activity restrictions. A follow-up is scheduled for suture removal and healing assessment.

After Your Extraction — What’s Next?

A missing tooth should be replaced to prevent neighbouring teeth from shifting, opposing teeth from over-erupting, and bone from resorbing. The three main replacement options are:

Dental Implant

The gold standard. A titanium root is placed in the bone and topped with a crown. Looks, feels, and functions like a natural tooth. Learn about implants or single-tooth implants.

Dental Bridge

A fixed prosthesis anchored to adjacent teeth. No surgery required, completed in two visits. Best when neighbouring teeth already have crowns. Learn about bridges.

Denture

A removable option for replacing one or multiple teeth. Suitable when implants or bridges are not feasible. Learn about dentures.

Socket Preservation for Future Implants

If you are considering an implant but are not ready for placement, ask Dr. Hussien about socket preservation bone grafting at the time of extraction. This one-step procedure fills the socket with graft material to maintain bone width and height, giving you the option to place an implant months later without needing a separate grafting surgery.

Recovery Timeline

Day 1 — Extraction Day

Bite on gauze for 30–45 minutes to control bleeding. Apply ice packs (20 minutes on, 20 minutes off) to reduce swelling. Stick to soft, cool foods. Avoid rinsing, spitting, or using a straw — these can dislodge the blood clot.

Days 2–3 — Swelling Peak

Swelling and mild bruising reach their peak. This is normal. Continue ice application. Begin gentle warm salt-water rinses (half teaspoon of salt in a glass of warm water) after 24 hours. Take prescribed pain medication and antibiotics as directed.

Days 4–7 — Improvement

Swelling subsides, discomfort reduces noticeably. Gradually return to normal foods, avoiding the extraction side. Continue salt-water rinses after meals. Most patients return to work or school within 2–3 days of a simple extraction.

Week 2 — Suture Removal

If sutures were placed, you return for removal (unless dissolvable sutures were used). Dr. Hussien checks healing progress and discusses the next steps for tooth replacement if applicable.

Common Questions

Tooth Extraction FAQs

How much does tooth extraction cost in Abu Dhabi?
At Vision Dental Clinic, simple tooth extraction starts from 179 AED. Moderate surgical extraction starts from 349 AED, and complex surgical extraction from 549 AED. The final cost depends on the tooth’s condition, root anatomy, and whether additional procedures like bone grafting are needed. We provide a clear cost estimate after your consultation and OPG panoramic assessment.
Is tooth extraction painful?
No. We use the STA (Single Tooth Anesthesia) system, which delivers computer-controlled local anesthesia for a virtually painless numbing experience. During the procedure you feel pressure but no pain. After the anesthesia wears off, mild discomfort is managed with prescribed pain medication.
How long does it take to recover from a tooth extraction?
Simple extractions: most patients feel back to normal within 2–3 days. Surgical extractions: expect 5–7 days for initial recovery, with swelling peaking on days 2–3 and resolving by day 5. Suture removal is typically at 7–10 days. Full bone healing takes 3–4 months.
Should I replace a tooth after extraction?
Yes, in most cases. A missing tooth causes neighbouring teeth to shift, the opposing tooth to over-erupt, and the jawbone to resorb over time. Replacement options include dental implants (the gold standard), dental bridges, or dentures. Wisdom teeth and orthodontic extractions do not need replacement.
What is dry socket and how do I prevent it?
Dry socket occurs when the blood clot in the extraction site is dislodged, exposing the underlying bone and nerve. It causes intense throbbing pain 2–4 days after extraction. Prevent it by avoiding rinsing, spitting, or using straws for 24 hours; not smoking; and following your aftercare instructions carefully. If it occurs, return to the clinic immediately for treatment.
Can I get a dental implant immediately after extraction?
In some cases, yes. Immediate implant placement is possible when the extraction site is free of infection and sufficient bone surrounds the socket. Dr. Hussien assesses eligibility during your consultation. If immediate placement is not suitable, socket preservation with bone grafting keeps the site ready for implant placement 3–4 months later.
