Restore your smile and chewing function with modern dentures — full, partial, or implant-retained. Custom-fitted by Dr. Emad El-Din Sharqawi, PhD Prosthodontics, at Vision Dental Clinic.

What Are Dentures?

Dentures are removable prosthetic devices that replace missing teeth and surrounding gum tissue. They restore your ability to eat, speak, and smile with confidence. Modern dentures are a far cry from the bulky, obvious appliances of the past — today’s materials and techniques produce dentures that look natural, fit comfortably, and function reliably.

You may need dentures if you have lost several teeth due to decay, gum disease, or trauma. Whether you are missing all teeth in one arch or just a few, there is a denture solution designed for your situation. Dr. Emad El-Din Sharqawi, our PhD prosthodontist, evaluates each case individually and recommends the type that delivers the best fit, function, and aesthetics.

Types of Dentures

Complete Dentures

Replace all teeth in the upper or lower arch. Made from acrylic resin with realistic-looking prosthetic teeth. Best for patients who are fully edentulous (no remaining natural teeth).

Partial Dentures

Replace some missing teeth while clasping onto your remaining natural teeth for support. A metal or flexible framework holds the prosthetic teeth in place. Ideal when you still have healthy teeth to anchor to.

Implant-Retained Overdentures

Snap onto 2–4 dental implants placed in the jawbone, providing far greater stability than conventional dentures. They eliminate slipping during eating and speaking and help preserve jawbone density.

Immediate Dentures

Placed on the same day as tooth extractions so you are never without teeth. These serve as a temporary solution while your gums heal and your permanent dentures are fabricated (typically 3–6 months).

Our Denture Process

Assessment & Treatment Planning

Dr. Emad examines your oral health, remaining teeth, gum condition, and jawbone. Digital photographs and impressions capture the starting point. If implant-retained dentures are suitable, implant planning begins here.

Impressions & Digital Scan

Precise impressions of your arches are taken — either with traditional moulds or a 3Shape intraoral scanner. These create an exact model of your mouth for the dental laboratory to work from.

Wax Try-In

A wax trial denture is created so you can preview the tooth arrangement, colour, shape, and bite before the final denture is made. Adjustments are easy at this stage — ensuring you are happy with the appearance and fit.

Fabrication

Once the wax try-in is approved, the laboratory fabricates your definitive denture using high-quality acrylic resin and prosthetic teeth matched to your natural shade and gum colour.

Delivery & Adjustments

Your finished denture is fitted, and Dr. Emad checks the bite, retention, and comfort. Minor adjustments are made chairside. Follow-up appointments ensure the fit remains comfortable as your tissues settle.

Implant-Retained vs Conventional Dentures

Conventional dentures rest on the gums and rely on suction (upper) or gravity and muscle control (lower) to stay in place. Over time, the jawbone beneath a conventional denture resorbs because it receives no stimulation — leading to a progressively looser fit, sore spots, and difficulty eating.

Implant-retained overdentures solve these problems. By placing 2–4 dental implants in the jaw, the denture snaps securely onto locator attachments. The result:

  • No slipping or clicking — the denture stays locked in place while you eat, speak, and laugh
  • Better chewing efficiency — bite force increases significantly compared to conventional dentures
  • Clearer speech — no movement means no mumbling or whistling caused by a shifting denture
  • Bone preservation — implants stimulate the jawbone, slowing the resorption that causes facial changes over time
  • Easy cleaning — the denture still removes for daily cleaning, and the implants are maintained like natural teeth

If you currently wear conventional dentures and struggle with looseness or discomfort, upgrading to an implant-retained overdenture can be life-changing. Dr. Emad will assess whether your bone density is sufficient or if bone grafting is needed first.

Denture Care

Daily Cleaning

Remove your dentures after every meal and rinse them under running water. Brush them daily with a soft denture brush and non-abrasive denture cleanser — never use regular toothpaste, which can scratch the acrylic surface and harbour bacteria.

Overnight Soaking

Most dentures should be removed at night to let your gums rest. Place them in a denture-soaking solution or plain water to prevent them from drying out and warping. Never use hot water — it can distort the shape.

Regular Relines

As your jawbone gradually changes shape, your denture’s fit loosens. A professional reline — adding new material to the denture base — restores the fit. Most patients need a reline every 1–2 years.

When to Replace

With proper care, a well-made denture lasts 5–8 years. Signs it is time for a replacement include persistent looseness despite relines, visible wear on the prosthetic teeth, cracks or chips in the base, and changes in your bite or facial appearance.

Important Reminder

Even with full dentures, you should visit Vision Dental at least once a year for an oral health check. Dr. Emad examines your gums, palate, and tongue for any changes, and assesses the condition and fit of your denture. Early detection of issues prevents discomfort and costly repairs.

Common Questions

Denture FAQs

How much do dentures cost in Abu Dhabi?
Denture costs vary depending on the type. Complete dentures are the most affordable, partial dentures are mid-range, and implant-retained overdentures carry a higher upfront cost due to the implant surgery. We provide a detailed cost breakdown after your consultation with Dr. Emad. Interest-free instalments are available through Tabby and Tamara.
How long does it take to get used to new dentures?
Most patients adjust within 2–4 weeks. During the first few days, you may experience increased saliva, minor soreness, and a feeling of fullness. Eating soft foods and practising speaking aloud helps speed up the adjustment period. Follow-up appointments with Dr. Emad ensure any sore spots are addressed promptly.
Can I eat normally with dentures?
Yes, but with some adaptation. Start with soft foods cut into small pieces and chew on both sides simultaneously. Over time, you can reintroduce firmer foods. Implant-retained overdentures offer significantly better chewing efficiency and allow you to eat most foods with confidence, including items that are difficult with conventional dentures.
How often do dentures need replacing?
A well-made denture typically lasts 5–8 years. However, your jawbone changes shape over time, so periodic relines (every 1–2 years) are needed to maintain the fit. If the denture develops cracks, significant wear, or persistent looseness despite relines, replacement is recommended.
What are implant-retained dentures?
Implant-retained dentures (overdentures) are removable dentures that snap onto 2–4 dental implants surgically placed in the jawbone. The implants act as anchors, preventing the denture from slipping or clicking. They provide superior stability, better chewing ability, and help preserve jawbone density compared to conventional dentures.
