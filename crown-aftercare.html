Dental Crown Aftercare & Longevity

Everything you need to know about caring for your dental crown — from temporary crown precautions to long-term maintenance that keeps your restoration lasting 10 to 15 years and beyond.

Temporary Crown Care

While your permanent crown is being fabricated, a temporary crown protects the prepared tooth. Temporary crowns are not as strong or well-fitted as permanent ones, so extra care is needed during this period.

  • Avoid sticky foods — caramel, toffee, chewing gum, and sticky candy can pull the temporary crown off the tooth
  • Chew on the other side — try to eat on the opposite side of your mouth to avoid dislodging the temporary crown
  • It may feel different — a temporary crown will not feel or look exactly like your final restoration. Some slight roughness or a different bite sensation is normal.
  • Be gentle when flossing — slide the floss out sideways rather than pulling it straight up, which can lift the temporary crown
  • Avoid hard foods — do not chew ice, nuts, or hard crusts on the temporary crown

If Your Temporary Crown Falls Off

Do not panic. Keep the crown safe and call the clinic as soon as possible to have it re-cemented. In the meantime, you can temporarily place it back on the tooth using a small amount of denture adhesive or toothpaste to hold it in place. Do not leave the prepared tooth exposed for more than a day or two, as it can become sensitive and the surrounding teeth may shift slightly.

After Your Permanent Crown Is Placed

Once your permanent crown is cemented, it will feel much more natural than the temporary. However, there is an adjustment period:

  • Sensitivity is normal — some sensitivity to hot, cold, or pressure is common for the first 1 to 2 weeks after placement. This should gradually diminish.
  • Bite adjustment — if your bite feels uneven or "high" when you close your teeth together, contact the clinic. A simple adjustment takes only a few minutes and makes a significant difference in comfort.
  • Bonding time — the cement sets quickly, but allow 24 hours before chewing heavily on the new crown. Stick to softer foods on the first day.
  • Gum tenderness — mild gum tenderness around the crown is normal for a few days after placement, especially if the gum tissue was moved during the procedure

Daily Care for Crowns

A dental crown does not require special products, but it does require consistent daily care. The crown itself cannot decay, but the natural tooth underneath it can — especially at the margin where the crown meets the tooth.

Brushing

Brush twice daily with a soft-bristle toothbrush. Pay particular attention to the gumline around the crown, where plaque and bacteria tend to accumulate. Use a non-abrasive fluoride toothpaste to protect both the crown surface and the natural tooth structure.

Flossing

Floss carefully around the crown margins every day. Slide the floss gently between the crown and the adjacent teeth, curving it in a C-shape around the base of the crown. This removes plaque from the area most vulnerable to decay and gum disease.

Non-Abrasive Toothpaste

Avoid highly abrasive whitening toothpastes, which can scratch the surface of some crown materials over time. Standard fluoride toothpaste is ideal for maintaining both the crown and the surrounding natural teeth.

Foods & Habits to Avoid

  • No chewing ice — ice is one of the most common causes of crown fractures. Never chew ice cubes.
  • No using teeth as tools — never use your crowned tooth to open packaging, tear tape, or hold objects. This applies to all teeth, but crowned teeth are especially vulnerable to fracture under sideways forces.
  • Avoid very sticky foods initially — for the first few weeks, avoid sticky candy, caramel, and toffee until you are confident the crown is fully settled
  • No biting nails — nail biting puts repeated lateral stress on crowns and can chip or crack the ceramic over time
  • No hard objects — do not bite pens, pencils, or other hard objects with your crowned tooth

Warning Signs

Contact the clinic if you notice any of the following:

  • Pain when biting — may indicate a bite that needs adjustment, a crack in the crown, or an issue with the underlying tooth
  • Crown feels loose — the cement may have washed out or failed. The crown needs to be re-cemented promptly to prevent decay underneath.
  • Dark line at the gumline — on PFM crowns, this may be the metal margin becoming visible as gums recede. On all-ceramic crowns, a dark line could indicate margin leakage.
  • Gum recession around the crown — the gum pulling away from the crown edge, exposing the root or margin beneath
  • Sensitivity that worsens — mild sensitivity after placement is normal, but sensitivity that develops or worsens months later may signal decay or nerve issues beneath the crown
  • Chipping or cracking — any visible damage to the crown surface should be assessed before it worsens

How Long Do Crowns Last?

With proper care, most dental crowns last 10 to 15 years, and many last significantly longer. The factors that affect crown longevity include:

Oral Hygiene

The most important factor. Poor hygiene leads to decay at the crown margin, which is the leading cause of crown failure.

Teeth Grinding

Bruxism generates forces far beyond normal chewing. Grinding can crack, chip, or loosen crowns prematurely. A night guard is essential for grinders.

Crown Material

Zirconia and E-max crowns are highly durable. PFM crowns are strong but the porcelain layer can chip. Material choice affects both aesthetics and longevity.

Night Guard Recommendation

If you grind or clench your teeth, a custom night guard is one of the most important investments you can make to protect your crown. Signs of bruxism include:

  • Jaw soreness or stiffness on waking
  • Morning headaches
  • Flat, worn, or chipped tooth edges
  • A partner noticing grinding sounds at night
  • Tooth sensitivity without an obvious cause

Your prosthodontist can assess your bite and fabricate a custom night guard that fits comfortably over your teeth and protects your crowns, veneers, and natural teeth from grinding damage.

Regular Check-Ups

Visit the clinic every 6 months for a professional cleaning and crown assessment. During these visits, your dentist will:

  • Check the integrity of the crown — margins, bonding, surface condition
  • Assess gum health around the crown
  • Look for signs of decay at the crown margin
  • Evaluate your bite and check for signs of grinding
  • Take any necessary scans if a concern is found

Most crown failures can be prevented or caught early at a routine check-up. Early intervention is always simpler and less costly than waiting for a problem to worsen.

Crown vs Veneer Aftercare

Crowns and veneers are both ceramic restorations, but they cover different amounts of the tooth and have slightly different care considerations.

Common Questions

Crown Aftercare FAQs

How long do dental crowns last?
Most dental crowns last 10 to 15 years with proper care, and many last significantly longer. The main factors affecting longevity are oral hygiene (especially at the crown margin), teeth grinding, the crown material, and regular dental check-ups. Zirconia and E-max crowns tend to be the most durable.
Is sensitivity after a crown normal?
Yes, mild sensitivity to hot, cold, or pressure is common for the first 1 to 2 weeks after a permanent crown is placed. This should gradually diminish. If sensitivity persists beyond two weeks or gets worse over time, contact the clinic for an assessment as it may indicate a bite issue or an underlying problem.
What should I do if my crown feels loose?
Call the clinic promptly. A loose crown means the cement has failed, and bacteria can reach the natural tooth underneath, causing decay. Do not try to re-cement it yourself with household adhesives. If the crown comes off completely, keep it safe, clean it, and bring it to your appointment. The crown can often be re-cemented if both the crown and tooth are undamaged.
Do I need a night guard with a crown?
If you grind or clench your teeth, a custom night guard is strongly recommended. Bruxism generates forces that can crack or chip crowns over time. Signs include jaw soreness on waking, morning headaches, and worn tooth edges. Your prosthodontist can assess whether you need one and fabricate a guard that fits comfortably.
Can I whiten my teeth if I have a crown?
Whitening treatments only affect natural tooth enamel — they do not change the colour of a crown. If you whiten your natural teeth, they may become lighter than your crown, creating a colour mismatch. If you are planning to whiten, discuss it with your dentist before getting a crown so the crown shade can be matched to your expected post-whitening colour.
