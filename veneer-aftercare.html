Veneer Aftercare & Longevity

Everything you need to know about caring for your veneers — daily routine, what to avoid, warning signs, and how to make them last 15 to 20 years.

How Long Do Veneers Last?

The lifespan of your veneers depends on two factors: the material used and how well you care for them.

Porcelain Veneers

15–20 years with proper care. Glazed ceramic resists staining and wear. E-max and Zirconia are both highly durable.

Composite Veneers

5–7 years on average. Composite resin is softer and more prone to staining and chipping, but is easy to repair.

The Key Factor

Aftercare is what separates veneers that last 10 years from veneers that last 20. Your daily habits matter more than the material alone.

The First 48 Hours

The bonding cement needs time to fully set. During the first two days after your veneers are placed:

  • Be gentle with your new veneers — chew carefully and avoid biting directly into hard foods
  • Avoid very hot or very cold foods and drinks, as sensitivity is common in the first 48 hours
  • Minor sensitivity to temperature and pressure is completely normal and will subside
  • Stick to softer foods for the first day — pasta, soup, scrambled eggs, yoghurt
  • If your bite feels uneven or "high," contact the clinic so we can adjust it

Daily Care Routine

Veneers do not require special products — but they do require consistent oral hygiene. Here is your daily routine:

Brushing

Brush twice daily (morning and night) with a soft-bristle toothbrush and a non-abrasive toothpaste. Avoid whitening toothpastes that contain harsh abrasives — they can scratch composite veneers and dull the polish over time. Fluoride toothpaste is recommended to protect the natural tooth structure beneath and around the veneer.

Flossing

Floss daily. Veneers do not change your flossing needs — plaque still builds up between teeth and at the gumline. Use regular floss or interdental brushes. If you find floss catches on a veneer margin, mention it at your next check-up.

Mouthwash (Optional)

An alcohol-free fluoride mouthwash is a helpful addition but not a substitute for brushing and flossing. Avoid mouthwashes with high alcohol content, which can weaken the bonding cement over time.

Do Not Use Your Teeth as Tools

This is the single most common cause of veneer damage. Never use your veneered teeth to:

  • Open packaging, bottles, or tags
  • Bite your fingernails
  • Tear tape or rip materials
  • Hold pens, pins, or other objects

Foods & Habits to Avoid

Hard Foods

Porcelain is strong but brittle under point forces. Avoid biting directly into:

  • Ice cubes — never chew ice
  • Hard candy and toffee
  • Nuts cracked with your teeth (shell-on pistachios, etc.)
  • Bone, cartilage, or hard crusts bitten with front teeth
  • Popcorn kernels

Staining Substances (Composite Veneers)

Porcelain veneers resist staining, but composite veneers are porous and can discolour over time. If you have composite veneers, limit:

  • Coffee and tea — drink through a straw when possible
  • Red wine and dark juices (grape, beetroot)
  • Turmeric, soy sauce, and dark curries
  • Smoking and tobacco use — the most significant staining risk

Teeth Grinding (Bruxism)

Bruxism is the biggest threat to veneer longevity. Grinding or clenching generates forces far beyond normal chewing — enough to chip, crack, or debond veneers. Many people grind in their sleep without knowing it.

Night Guard Recommendation

If you grind or clench your teeth, a custom night guard is essential to protect your veneers. Signs of bruxism include jaw soreness on waking, headaches, flat or worn tooth edges, and a partner noticing grinding sounds at night. Dr. Ammar can assess your bite and fabricate a protective night guard that fits over your veneers without damaging them. This is one of the most important investments you can make in the longevity of your smile.

Warning Signs to Watch For

Check your veneers regularly in good light. Contact the clinic if you notice any of the following:

  • Chipping or cracking — even a small chip should be assessed before it worsens
  • A veneer feels loose — the bonding may have failed; it needs to be re-cemented promptly
  • Margin discolouration — a dark line at the edge where the veneer meets the tooth, suggesting cement breakdown or leakage
  • Gum recession — the gum pulling away from the veneer edge, exposing the natural tooth root beneath
  • Increased sensitivity — sensitivity that develops months or years after placement may indicate an underlying issue
  • A rough or sharp edge — the veneer surface may have worn or fractured in a small area

When to See Your Dentist

Do not wait for your next routine appointment if:

  • A veneer chips, cracks, or breaks
  • A veneer feels loose or comes off completely (keep it safe and bring it to the clinic)
  • You develop new or worsening sensitivity
  • Your gums bleed, swell, or recede around a veneer
  • You notice a dark line at the margin of a veneer

Early intervention is always simpler and less costly than waiting for a small problem to become a large one.

Regular Check-Ups

Visit the clinic every 6 months for a professional cleaning and veneer assessment. During these visits, your dentist will:

  • Check the integrity of each veneer — margins, bonding, surface condition
  • Assess your gum health around the veneers
  • Polish the veneers to restore lustre (especially composite)
  • Identify early signs of wear, bruxism, or bonding failure
  • Take any necessary scans if a concern is found

These visits are the foundation of veneer longevity. Most veneer failures that we see could have been prevented or caught early at a routine check-up.

Can Veneers Be Repaired?

It depends on the material and the extent of the damage:

ScenarioPorcelain VeneersComposite Veneers
Minor chipCan sometimes be polished and smoothed. Minor composite bonding may disguise the chip.Easily repaired chairside with fresh composite resin in one visit.
Major chip or crackUsually requires full replacement of the veneer.May be repairable if the remaining veneer is intact; otherwise replaced.
Debonding (veneer comes off)Can often be re-bonded if the veneer and tooth are undamaged.New composite is applied directly; the old material is removed.
StainingPorcelain does not stain. Margin staining requires professional cleaning or re-cementing.Professional polishing helps. Deep staining may require replacement.

When Veneers Need Replacement

Even well-maintained veneers will eventually reach the end of their service life. Signs that replacement may be needed:

  • Margin leakage — persistent dark lines or staining at the veneer edge that cannot be cleaned
  • Major chip or fracture — a break that cannot be polished or repaired
  • Significant gum recession — exposing the natural tooth below the veneer margin, creating an aesthetic gap
  • Colour mismatch — the veneer shade no longer matches your other teeth (common after whitening adjacent teeth)
  • Underlying decay — if decay develops at the veneer margin, the veneer must be removed, the decay treated, and a new veneer placed

Replacement follows the same process as your original veneers — preparation, impression, fabrication, and bonding. Our in-house lab means the turnaround remains fast.

Common Questions

Veneer Aftercare FAQs

How long do porcelain veneers last?
Porcelain veneers typically last 15 to 20 years with proper care. The main factors that shorten their lifespan are teeth grinding (bruxism), using teeth as tools, and poor oral hygiene that causes margin breakdown. Regular 6-monthly check-ups help catch issues early.
Can I drink coffee with veneers?
With porcelain veneers, yes — glazed ceramic is highly stain-resistant and coffee will not discolour the surface. With composite veneers, coffee can cause gradual staining over time. Using a straw and rinsing with water after drinking can help reduce staining on composite veneers.
Do I need a night guard with veneers?
If you grind or clench your teeth (bruxism), a custom night guard is strongly recommended. Bruxism generates forces that can chip or crack veneers over time. Many people grind in their sleep without realising it. Your dentist can assess whether you show signs of bruxism and fabricate a guard that fits comfortably over your veneers.
What happens if a veneer chips?
A small chip in a porcelain veneer can sometimes be polished smooth or disguised with a minor composite repair. Composite veneers are easy to repair chairside with fresh resin. A major chip or crack in porcelain usually means the veneer needs to be replaced entirely. Either way, see your dentist promptly — leaving a chip untreated can lead to further damage.
How often should I visit the dentist with veneers?
Every 6 months for a professional cleaning and veneer assessment. Your dentist checks the margins, bonding integrity, gum health, and surface condition of each veneer. These routine visits are the single best way to extend the life of your veneers and catch problems before they become costly.
