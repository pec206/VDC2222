Can Receding Gums
Grow Back?

What Is Gum Recession?

Gum recession happens when the gum tissue that surrounds your teeth gradually pulls back or wears away, exposing more of the tooth -- or even the tooth root. It is a common dental problem that develops slowly, so many people do not notice it until it becomes significant. When gums recede, gaps or "pockets" form between the teeth and the gum line, making it easier for bacteria to build up and cause damage.

Recession does not just affect the way your smile looks. Exposed tooth roots are softer than enamel-covered crowns. They are more vulnerable to decay, more sensitive to temperature changes, and more likely to cause discomfort when you eat or drink. Left untreated, severe gum recession can eventually lead to tooth loss.

Understanding why gums recede, what treatments are available, and how to prevent further damage can help you protect your oral health for years to come.

Common Causes of Gum Recession

Gum recession rarely has a single cause. In most cases, it results from a combination of factors. Here are the most common reasons gums pull away from the teeth:

Aggressive Brushing

Brushing too hard or using a stiff-bristled toothbrush is one of the leading causes of recession. When you scrub your teeth with excessive force, you gradually wear away the delicate gum tissue. Over months and years, the damage adds up. This type of recession often appears on the side of the mouth corresponding to the hand you brush with.

Gum Disease (Periodontal Disease)

Bacterial gum infections destroy gum tissue and the bone that supports your teeth. Periodontitis is the most common cause of gum recession worldwide. The bacteria in plaque trigger an inflammatory response that breaks down the attachment between the gum and the tooth, causing the tissue to pull away. If you notice bleeding, swelling, or persistent bad breath alongside recession, gum disease may be the underlying problem. Learn more about periodontal treatment options.

Teeth Grinding and Clenching (Bruxism)

If you grind or clench your teeth -- especially at night -- the excessive force puts constant stress on your teeth and gums. Over time, this pressure can cause the gum tissue to recede. Bruxism is often linked to stress, sleep disorders, or bite misalignment, and many people are not even aware they do it until a dentist identifies the signs.

Genetics

Some people are simply more predisposed to gum recession. Research shows that up to 30% of the population may be genetically susceptible to gum disease regardless of how well they care for their teeth. If your parents experienced receding gums, you may be at higher risk.

Other Contributing Factors

  • Misaligned teeth or bite problems: When teeth do not come together evenly, uneven force on certain areas can accelerate recession.
  • Hormonal changes: Fluctuations during pregnancy, puberty, and menopause can make gums more sensitive and vulnerable to recession.
  • Tobacco use: Smoking or chewing tobacco promotes plaque buildup that is difficult to remove and impairs gum healing.
  • Lip or tongue piercings: Jewellery can rub against and irritate gum tissue, wearing it away over time.
  • Poor oral hygiene: When plaque is not removed through regular brushing and flossing, it hardens into tartar, which pushes gums away from the teeth.

Can Receding Gums Grow Back?

This is the question most patients ask, and the honest answer is: no, gums do not grow back on their own. Unlike some tissues in the body, gum tissue does not regenerate once it has been lost. Once the gums have pulled away from the teeth, they will not return to their original position without professional intervention.

However, the situation is not hopeless. While natural regrowth is not possible, modern dental treatments can restore gum coverage, protect exposed roots, and prevent further recession. The key is to act early -- the sooner recession is addressed, the more treatment options are available and the better the outcomes tend to be.

Gum tissue does not regenerate naturally, but professional treatment can restore coverage and stop further damage. Early action gives you the best results.

Treatment Options for Receding Gums

The right treatment depends on the severity of the recession, the underlying cause, and your overall oral health. Here are the main approaches used at Vision Dental Clinic:

Deep Cleaning (Scaling and Root Planing)

For mild recession caused by gum disease, a deep cleaning may be the first step. During scaling and root planing, the dentist carefully removes plaque and tartar buildup from below the gum line. The tooth root surfaces are then smoothed to help the gums reattach to the teeth. This treatment addresses the bacterial infection driving the recession and can halt further gum loss when combined with improved home care.

Gum Grafting

Gum grafting is the most established surgical treatment for moderate to severe recession. There are several types:

  • Connective tissue graft: A small piece of tissue is taken from under a flap in the roof of your mouth and stitched over the exposed root. This is the most common type of gum graft.
  • Gingival graft: Tissue is taken directly from the palate (rather than from under a flap) and attached to the receded area. This method is often used for patients with naturally thin gums.
  • Pedicle graft: Instead of taking tissue from the palate, the dentist creates a flap from gum tissue near the affected tooth and stretches it over the exposed root. This option works best when there is plenty of gum tissue near the recession site.

Gum grafting can restore a natural gum line, reduce sensitivity, protect roots from decay, and improve the appearance of your smile. Recovery typically takes one to two weeks, and the results are long-lasting. Explore our cosmetic gum treatments for more details.

Pinhole Surgical Technique

The pinhole technique is a newer, minimally invasive approach to treating gum recession. Instead of cutting and grafting tissue, the dentist makes a tiny pinhole in the gum tissue above the receded area. Using special instruments, the existing gum tissue is gently loosened and guided down over the exposed root. Collagen strips are placed through the pinhole to stabilise the gum in its new position.

Benefits of the pinhole technique include:

  • No scalpel incisions or sutures
  • Minimal discomfort during and after the procedure
  • Faster recovery compared to traditional grafting
  • Multiple teeth can be treated in a single session
  • Natural-looking, long-lasting results

Bonding and Veneers

In some cases, tooth-coloured composite resin can be applied over exposed roots to reduce sensitivity and improve appearance. While this does not restore gum tissue, it can protect the root surface and make the recession less noticeable. For front teeth, porcelain veneers may also be considered as part of a broader cosmetic treatment plan.

How to Prevent Further Gum Recession

Whether you have already experienced some recession or want to avoid it entirely, these habits can protect your gum health:

  • Use a soft-bristled toothbrush: Replace your toothbrush every three months and choose one with soft bristles. An electric toothbrush with a pressure sensor is even better -- it alerts you if you are brushing too hard.
  • Brush gently: Use small circular motions rather than back-and-forth scrubbing. Let the bristles do the work without pressing down hard.
  • Floss daily: Removing plaque from between teeth and below the gum line prevents the bacterial buildup that leads to gum disease and recession.
  • Attend regular dental check-ups: Professional cleanings every six months remove tartar that home brushing cannot reach. Your dentist can also spot early signs of recession before it becomes severe.
  • Quit smoking: Tobacco products contribute to plaque buildup and impair your body's ability to heal gum tissue.
  • Wear a night guard: If you grind your teeth, a custom night guard distributes the force evenly and protects your gums and teeth from excessive pressure.
  • Address bite issues: Crooked teeth or a misaligned bite can create uneven pressure that accelerates recession. Orthodontic treatment can help distribute forces more evenly.
  • Eat a balanced diet: Foods rich in vitamin C, calcium, and antioxidants support gum health and tissue repair.

When to See a Dentist

You should book a dental appointment if you notice any of the following signs:

  • Your teeth look longer than they used to
  • You can feel a notch or ridge near the gum line with your fingernail
  • Your teeth are becoming increasingly sensitive to hot, cold, or sweet foods
  • Your gums bleed when you brush or floss
  • You notice gaps forming between your teeth near the gum line
  • Your gums appear red, swollen, or puffy along certain teeth

Early detection is critical. Mild recession can often be managed with non-surgical treatments, but waiting too long may mean more invasive procedures become necessary. Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Why are my gums receding?
There are multiple causes of gum recession, including aggressive brushing, gum disease, teeth grinding, aging, and genetic factors. A dental examination can identify the specific cause in your case.
Is gum recession serious?
Yes, gum recession is a serious condition. It exposes the tooth roots, which are more prone to decay and sensitivity. Without treatment, recession can lead to tooth loss.
Can receding gums be reversed?
Natural regrowth of gum tissue is not possible. However, treatments such as gum grafting and the pinhole surgical technique can restore gum coverage and protect exposed roots.
How can I stop gum recession?
Treat the underlying cause first. Switch to a soft-bristled toothbrush, avoid aggressive brushing, attend regular dental check-ups, and address any gum disease or grinding habits.
Does gum recession cause sensitivity?
Yes. Exposed tooth roots lack the protective enamel covering that crowns have, making them significantly more sensitive to hot, cold, sweet, and acidic foods and drinks.
