When Wisdom Teeth Need Removal

Wisdom teeth (third molars) are the last teeth to emerge, usually between ages 17 and 25. When there is not enough room in the jaw, they become impacted — trapped beneath gum tissue or bone. Impacted wisdom teeth can cause pain, infection, damage to neighbouring teeth, and cysts. Even wisdom teeth that erupt partially often create pockets where food and bacteria collect, leading to recurring infections (pericoronitis).

Common signs that a wisdom tooth needs removal:

  • Pain or throbbing at the back of the jaw, often radiating to the ear or temple
  • Swollen, red, or tender gums around the partially erupted tooth
  • Difficulty opening the mouth (trismus) or pain when chewing
  • Recurring infection or abscess in the gum tissue overlying the tooth
  • Crowding or shifting of adjacent teeth as the wisdom tooth pushes forward
  • Cyst or tumour formation around an unerupted wisdom tooth (visible on OPG panorama)

Types of Impaction

Wisdom teeth can be impacted in different orientations, each affecting the difficulty of extraction:

Mesioangular

The tooth is angled forward toward the second molar. This is the most common type of impaction and often causes pressure on the adjacent tooth.

Distoangular

The tooth is angled backward toward the ramus (back of the jaw). Less common but can be more complex to extract surgically.

Horizontal

The tooth lies completely on its side, pressing directly into the roots of the second molar. Usually requires surgical removal with bone removal.

Vertical

The tooth is in the correct upright position but lacks space to erupt through the gum. May erupt partially, causing a gum flap that traps bacteria.

The Extraction Process

Consultation & OPG Panorama

Dr. Hussien Tahoun examines the area clinically and takes an OPG panoramic radiograph to visualise the tooth position, root morphology, and proximity to the inferior alveolar nerve. The extraction plan — simple or surgical — is determined at this stage.

STA Digital Anesthesia

We use the STA (Single Tooth Anesthesia) system — a computer-controlled injection that delivers anesthetic at a slow, controlled rate. This eliminates the sharp sting of traditional injections and provides profound numbness targeted to the exact tooth being treated.

Extraction Technique

For simple extractions, the tooth is loosened with elevators and removed with forceps. For surgical cases, a small gum flap is raised, bone is carefully removed using piezoelectric instruments (ultrasonic vibration that cuts bone precisely without damaging soft tissue or nerves), and the tooth is sectioned if needed for a less traumatic removal.

Suturing

Surgical extraction sites are closed with resorbable sutures that dissolve on their own within 7–10 days. Simple extractions may not require sutures. A gauze pad is placed over the socket to control bleeding and encourage clot formation.

Post-Operative Instructions

You receive written aftercare instructions, prescribed medications (antibiotics if indicated, anti-inflammatory, and pain relief), and a direct contact number for Dr. Hussien’s team in case of any concerns during recovery.

Recovery & Aftercare

Most patients return to normal activities within 3–5 days after wisdom tooth extraction. The timeline below covers what to expect:

First 24 Hours

Bite on gauze for 30–45 minutes. Apply ice packs (20 minutes on, 20 off). Eat soft, cold foods only. Do not spit, rinse, or use a straw — this can dislodge the blood clot and cause dry socket.

Days 2–3

Swelling peaks on day 2–3 and then begins to subside. Continue ice packs and prescribed medications. Gently rinse with warm salt water after meals. Eat soft foods (soup, yoghurt, mashed potato).

Days 4–7

Swelling and bruising resolve. Most patients can return to work or school by day 3–4. Gradually reintroduce firmer foods as comfort allows. Continue gentle rinsing.

Week 2

The extraction site is largely healed on the surface. Resorbable sutures dissolve. Full bone healing beneath the gum takes 6–8 weeks. A follow-up visit confirms everything is healing normally.

When to Call the Clinic

Contact us immediately if you experience: severe pain that worsens after day 3 (possible dry socket), uncontrolled bleeding that does not stop with pressure, fever above 38°C, pus or foul taste from the extraction site, or numbness in your lip or tongue that persists beyond 24 hours after the anesthetic has worn off. Common Questions

Wisdom Tooth FAQs

How much does wisdom tooth extraction cost in Abu Dhabi?
At Vision Dental Clinic, simple wisdom tooth extraction starts from 179 AED. Moderate surgical extraction (soft tissue or partial bony impaction) starts from 349 AED, and complex surgical extraction (full bony impaction) starts from 549 AED. The exact cost depends on the type of impaction, which Dr. Hussien Tahoun determines from your OPG panoramic radiograph during consultation.
Is wisdom tooth extraction painful?
No. We use STA (Single Tooth Anesthesia) — a computer-controlled injection system that delivers anesthetic slowly and precisely, eliminating the sharp sting of a traditional injection. You will feel pressure during the extraction but no pain. Post-operative discomfort is managed with prescribed medications and typically resolves within 3–5 days.
How long does recovery take after wisdom tooth removal?
Most patients return to normal activities within 3–5 days. Swelling peaks on days 2–3 and subsides by day 5–7. Simple extractions recover faster than surgical ones. Full bone healing beneath the gum takes 6–8 weeks, but this does not affect your daily routine.
What is dry socket and how do I prevent it?
Dry socket (alveolar osteitis) occurs when the blood clot in the extraction site is dislodged, exposing the bone underneath. It causes intense, throbbing pain starting 2–4 days after extraction. To prevent it: do not spit, rinse forcefully, or use a straw for the first 24 hours; avoid smoking for at least 72 hours; and follow all post-operative instructions provided by Dr. Hussien.
Do all wisdom teeth need to be removed?
No. If your wisdom teeth have fully erupted in proper alignment, are healthy, and can be cleaned effectively with regular brushing and flossing, they do not need removal. Extraction is recommended when they are impacted, causing pain or infection, damaging adjacent teeth, or contributing to cyst formation. Dr. Hussien assesses each case with an OPG panoramic radiograph.
Can I get a dental implant after wisdom tooth extraction?
Wisdom teeth are rarely replaced with implants because they are not essential for chewing. However, if an adjacent tooth (such as a second molar) was damaged and needs extraction, a dental implant can be placed after healing. In such cases, Dr. Hussien can perform socket preservation with a bone graft at the time of extraction to maintain bone volume for the future implant.
