Can TMJ Problems
Cause Headaches?

The Headache That Starts in Your Jaw

You have had the headache for weeks. It settles around your temples, radiates behind your eyes, and tightens across the side of your face. Painkillers dull it temporarily, but it keeps coming back. You have seen your doctor, maybe even a neurologist. But no one has asked you about your jaw.

For many people, chronic headaches are caused -- or significantly worsened -- by TMJ dysfunction. The jaw joint sits millimetres from the temporal bone, and the muscles that control it fan across the side of the head. When those muscles are strained, overworked, or in spasm, the pain radiates upward -- producing what feels exactly like a tension headache or even a migraine.

How the Jaw Causes Head Pain

The connection between the TMJ and headaches is not a coincidence of location. It runs through shared anatomy:

The Temporalis Muscle

This large, fan-shaped muscle stretches from the jaw all the way up to the temple. It is one of the primary muscles used for clenching and chewing. When it is overworked -- from grinding, clenching, or a bite that forces it to compensate -- it goes into spasm. The result is a dull, pressing pain across the temple that can last for hours.

The Trigeminal Nerve

The trigeminal nerve is the largest cranial nerve serving the face, and it has three branches. One of those branches innervates the jaw joint and the muscles of mastication. Another serves the forehead and the area around the eyes. When the jaw branch is irritated by TMJ problems, pain signals can radiate along the other branches -- producing headaches, eye pain, and even sinus-like pressure that have nothing to do with the sinuses.

The Masseter and Medial Pterygoid

These powerful chewing muscles sit along the sides and inside of the jaw. Trigger points in these muscles -- areas of sustained contraction -- refer pain to the temples, the ears, and behind the eyes. The pain pattern mimics tension-type headaches so closely that many people are treated for headaches for years without anyone examining the jaw.

What TMJ Headaches Feel Like

TMJ-related headaches have specific characteristics that set them apart from other types, though they can overlap:

  • Location: Usually at the temples, behind the eyes, or along the side of the head -- one side or both
  • Timing: Often worst in the morning (from nighttime clenching) or at the end of the day (from daytime stress and jaw tension)
  • Aggravating factors: Worsened by chewing, talking for long periods, wide opening, or stressful situations
  • Accompanying signs: Jaw clicking or popping, jaw stiffness, difficulty opening the mouth fully, ear fullness or ringing
  • Duration: Can be daily or near-daily, lasting hours at a time
  • Response to painkillers: Partially or temporarily relieved, but always returns

If your headaches come with jaw symptoms -- even mild ones like occasional clicking or morning jaw stiffness -- the connection is worth investigating. The jaw is one of the most overlooked causes of chronic headache.

TMJ and Migraines -- Are They Connected?

Research increasingly supports a strong link between TMJ disorders and migraines. The trigeminal nerve is the primary pathway for migraine pain, and it is the same nerve irritated by TMJ dysfunction. This means:

  • TMJ problems can trigger migraines in people who are predisposed to them
  • TMJ dysfunction can increase the frequency of migraine episodes
  • Treating the TMJ problem can reduce migraine frequency and severity even when it does not eliminate migraines entirely

This does not mean every migraine is caused by the jaw. But in patients who have both migraines and TMJ symptoms, addressing the jaw component often produces a significant reduction in overall headache burden.

Why TMJ Headaches Are Often Misdiagnosed

TMJ headaches fly under the radar for several reasons:

  • They mimic other conditions: The pain pattern is nearly identical to tension headaches, and the ear and sinus symptoms overlap with ENT conditions
  • Medical training gaps: Headaches are typically evaluated by neurologists and general practitioners, who may not examine the jaw
  • Gradual onset: Patients adapt to mild jaw symptoms and stop noticing them, so they do not mention them
  • Separate specialists: The head is treated by one doctor and the teeth by another, so the connection between the two can fall through the cracks

This is why a dental evaluation is an important step for anyone with persistent headaches that do not respond fully to standard treatment.

How We Diagnose TMJ-Related Headaches

At Vision Dental Clinic, Dr. Hussien Tahoun evaluates the jaw systematically to determine whether it is contributing to headache symptoms:

  • Muscle palpation: Checking the temporalis, masseter, and pterygoid muscles for tenderness, trigger points, and spasm
  • Joint examination: Listening for clicking or crepitus, measuring opening range, and testing for pain during jaw movements
  • Bite assessment: Evaluating how the teeth come together and whether premature contacts or misalignment are straining the joint
  • CBCT imaging: When needed, a 3D scan reveals the condition of the joint structures, including disc position and bone changes
  • Symptom correlation: Mapping headache patterns to jaw habits, stress levels, and sleep quality

Treatment That Targets the Cause

When a TMJ component is confirmed, treatment focuses on reducing the strain on the jaw that is driving the headache cycle:

Custom Occlusal Splint

A precision-made night guard that repositions the jaw and dramatically reduces clenching force. For patients whose headaches are worst in the morning, this alone can produce noticeable improvement within the first two weeks.

Jaw Muscle Therapy

Targeted exercises that stretch and relax the temporalis and masseter muscles, combined with self-massage techniques for trigger points. These are simple, done at home, and highly effective at reducing referred pain.

Stress and Habit Awareness

Many patients clench their jaw without realising -- during work, while driving, during exercise. Simply becoming aware of the habit and consciously relaxing the jaw throughout the day can break the muscle tension cycle that fuels headaches.

Bite Correction

If a bite imbalance is forcing the muscles to compensate, correcting it through selective adjustment or orthodontic treatment removes the root cause rather than just managing symptoms.

Lifestyle Modifications

Avoiding excessively hard or chewy foods, applying warm compresses to the jaw, improving sleep posture, and managing stress all support the treatment and reduce headache recurrence.

When your headache is coming from your jaw, no amount of headache medication will make it go away permanently. Treating the source -- the TMJ -- is what breaks the cycle.

When to See a Dentist About Your Headaches

Consider a TMJ evaluation if:

  • You have headaches that do not fully respond to medication
  • Your headaches are accompanied by jaw pain, clicking, or stiffness
  • You wake up with headaches or facial pain
  • You know you grind or clench your teeth
  • Your headaches get worse with chewing or talking
  • You have been told you have tension headaches but treatment has not resolved them

A TMJ assessment at Vision Dental Clinic can determine whether your jaw is contributing to your headaches -- and if it is, treatment can begin the same visit.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if my headache is from TMJ?
TMJ headaches typically start near the temples or behind the eyes, worsen with jaw use (chewing, talking), and are accompanied by jaw pain, clicking, or stiffness. A dental evaluation can confirm the connection.
Can TMJ problems trigger migraines?
Yes. TMJ dysfunction can trigger or worsen migraines through shared nerve pathways. The trigeminal nerve serves both the jaw joint and much of the head and face.
Can a night guard help with TMJ headaches?
Yes. A custom occlusal splint reduces clenching force and jaw strain during sleep, which is a major trigger for morning headaches related to TMJ.
Can fixing my bite stop TMJ headaches?
If a bite imbalance is the underlying cause, correcting it through orthodontics or occlusal adjustment can significantly reduce or eliminate TMJ-related headaches.
How long does it take for TMJ headaches to improve with treatment?
Many patients notice improvement within 2 to 4 weeks of consistent splint use. Full resolution may take 2 to 3 months depending on the severity.
