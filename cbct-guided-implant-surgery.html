CBCT-Guided Implant Surgery

3D imaging and digital surgical guides remove the guesswork from implant placement — every implant is planned on a computer before it's placed in your jaw.

What Is CBCT?

CBCT (Cone Beam Computed Tomography) is a 3D X-ray technology specifically designed for dental and maxillofacial imaging. Unlike a standard dental X-ray that produces a flat 2D image, a CBCT scan creates a complete three-dimensional model of your jaw, teeth, nerves, sinuses, and bone.

The scan takes about 15 seconds. You stand still while the machine rotates once around your head. The result is a detailed 3D dataset that can be viewed from any angle and measured precisely in millimetres.

3D Bone Mapping

See exact bone height, width, and density at the planned implant site — measurements not possible on a 2D X-ray.

Nerve Localisation

The inferior alveolar nerve (lower jaw) is mapped precisely, preventing nerve injury during placement.

Sinus Proximity

Upper jaw implants require knowing the exact distance to the sinus floor — CBCT measures this to the millimetre.

Low Radiation

A dental CBCT delivers a fraction of the radiation of a medical CT scan — comparable to a few standard dental X-rays.

Why Guided Surgery Matters

Implant success depends on placing the implant in the right position, at the right angle, at the right depth. A millimetre of deviation can mean the difference between a well-supported crown and a compromised result.

In freehand surgery, the surgeon relies on experience and visual assessment. In guided surgery, the implant position is planned digitally and transferred to the patient's mouth using a physical surgical guide — a custom-printed template that fits over the teeth or gums and directs the drill to the exact planned position.

Guided vs. Freehand

Freehand Placement

  • Surgeon estimates position visually
  • Angle and depth based on experience
  • Less predictable in complex cases
  • Relies on 2D X-ray planning
  • Adequate for simple, single-tooth cases

CBCT-Guided Placement

  • Position planned digitally in 3D
  • Custom surgical guide controls drill path
  • Predictable results in complex anatomy
  • Full 3D bone and nerve mapping
  • Essential for full-arch and immediate cases

When Is Guided Surgery Used?

Dr. Hussien uses CBCT planning for every implant case. A physical surgical guide is fabricated for complex cases: multiple implants, full-arch rehabilitation, limited bone, proximity to the nerve canal or sinus, and immediate placement. For straightforward single-tooth cases with ample bone, the CBCT plan may be executed freehand — the 3D data still informs the placement even without a guide.

The Digital Workflow

CBCT Scan

A 3D scan of your jaw is captured in our clinic. The scan reveals bone volume, density, nerve pathways, sinus position, and any existing pathology.

Digital Planning

Dr. Hussien imports the CBCT data into implant planning software. The ideal implant position, angle, and depth are determined based on the available bone, the nerve and sinus boundaries, and the final prosthetic design (where the crown needs to be).

Surgical Guide Fabrication

The digital plan is converted into a physical surgical guide — a 3D-printed template with metal sleeves that fit over the planned implant sites. The guide snaps onto the patient's teeth or gums and constrains the drill to the exact planned trajectory.

Guided Surgery

During surgery, the guide is seated in the mouth. The surgeon drills through the guide sleeves, which restrict the drill's position, angle, and depth to the digital plan. The implant is placed through the guide into the prepared site.

Verification

A post-operative scan or X-ray confirms the implant is in the planned position. For immediate loading cases, the pre-planned temporary prosthesis is attached.

Clinical Benefits

  • Accuracy: Published literature reports implant placement within 1–2mm of the planned position when using surgical guides, compared to greater variability in freehand placement.
  • Safety: Nerve injury, sinus perforation, and fenestration (drilling through the bone wall) are significantly less likely when anatomy is mapped in 3D and the drill path is constrained.
  • Efficiency: Surgery is often faster because the guesswork is eliminated — drill sites are pre-planned and the guide directs every step.
  • Prosthetic-driven placement: The implant is positioned where the final crown needs it to be, not just where there happens to be bone. This produces better-looking, better-functioning results.
  • Minimally invasive: Guided surgery can sometimes be performed flapless (without lifting the gum), reducing post-operative swelling and healing time.

Who Benefits Most?

While CBCT planning benefits every implant patient, guided surgery is particularly valuable for:

  • Full-arch (All-on-4 / All-on-6) cases — multiple implants must be precisely angled to maximise bone contact and support a full bridge. Learn more →
  • Immediate implants — placement into an extraction socket requires exact positioning to achieve primary stability. Learn more →
  • Limited bone — when bone grafting is needed, or when implants must thread between the nerve canal and the sinus floor.
  • Aesthetic zone — front teeth demand precise angulation for proper emergence profile and gum symmetry.
  • Medically complex patients — shorter, more predictable surgery reduces anaesthesia time and post-operative risk.

Common Questions

CBCT & Guided Surgery FAQs

Is CBCT safe? How much radiation is involved?
A dental CBCT scan delivers significantly less radiation than a medical CT scan — typically equivalent to 4–8 standard dental X-rays. The scan takes about 15 seconds. It is considered safe for dental diagnostic purposes and is only taken when clinically indicated.
Does every implant patient need a CBCT scan?
At Vision Dental, yes. Dr. Hussien requires a CBCT scan for every implant case. A 2D X-ray cannot show bone width, density, or the exact relationship to nerves and sinuses. The 3D data is essential for safe, accurate planning — whether the case uses a physical surgical guide or not.
What is a surgical guide?
A surgical guide is a 3D-printed template that fits over your teeth or gums during implant surgery. It has metal sleeves at the planned implant positions that constrain the drill — controlling position, angle, and depth so the implant goes exactly where it was digitally planned.
Does guided surgery cost extra?
The CBCT scan and digital planning are included in the standard implant assessment at Vision Dental. When a physical surgical guide is fabricated, there is an additional cost for the guide. This is discussed during your consultation and included in your treatment plan. See pricing details →
Can you do the CBCT scan at the clinic?
Yes. Vision Dental has an in-house CBCT scanner. The scan is taken during your consultation appointment — no need to visit a separate imaging centre. Results are available immediately for Dr. Hussien to review with you.
