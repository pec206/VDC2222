Dental Implant vs Bridge

Both replace missing teeth — but they differ in how they do it, what they cost long-term, and how they affect the teeth around them. Here's an honest clinical comparison.

The Core Difference

A dental implant replaces the tooth root with a titanium post embedded in the jawbone, topped with a crown. It stands alone — nothing touches the adjacent teeth.

A dental bridge (3-unit fixed bridge) spans the gap by crowning the two adjacent teeth and suspending a false tooth between them. It requires filing down those two healthy teeth to serve as anchors.

When Is an Implant Better?

An implant is the preferred choice in most clinical situations:

  • Adjacent teeth are healthy — grinding them down for a bridge removes healthy tooth structure permanently
  • Long-term value matters — implants outlast bridges and don't put adjacent teeth at risk
  • Bone preservation is important — especially for younger patients who face decades of potential bone loss
  • You want easy maintenance — implants are cleaned like natural teeth with no special tools

When Is a Bridge Better?

There are specific clinical situations where a bridge makes more sense:

  • Adjacent teeth already need crowns — if the neighboring teeth are heavily restored or damaged, crowning them for a bridge treats them while filling the gap
  • Medical contraindications — certain health conditions or medications may make implant surgery inadvisable
  • Insufficient bone and grafting is declined — if bone volume is inadequate and you prefer not to undergo grafting
  • Speed is critical — a bridge can be completed in 2–3 weeks versus 3–4 months for an implant

The Long-Term Reality

A bridge that fails after 12 years leaves you with two compromised teeth and a gap. At that point, you may still need implants — plus treatment for the damaged anchor teeth.

An implant that needs attention after 15+ years typically only requires a new crown (not a new implant). The adjacent teeth remain untouched throughout.

This is why many prosthodontists and oral surgeons — including Dr. Hussien — recommend implants as the first-line treatment when the clinical situation allows it.

Cost Comparison

The upfront costs are often closer than patients expect:

  • Single implant: from 2,600 AED (implant + abutment + crown)
  • 3-unit bridge: three crown preparations and fabrications — often comparable in total

The long-term cost favours implants because bridges typically need replacement once or twice over a lifetime, while implants rarely need more than a crown update. See our full implant pricing →

Implant vs Bridge FAQs

Is an implant always better than a bridge?
Not always. If the adjacent teeth already need crowns, a bridge treats multiple problems at once. And some medical conditions may make surgery inadvisable. But when adjacent teeth are healthy, an implant is the more conservative and longer-lasting choice.
Can I switch from a bridge to an implant later?
Yes, though bone loss under the bridge may mean a bone graft is needed first. The anchor teeth will also still have their modified (filed-down) shape from the bridge preparation — they'll need new individual crowns.
Which lasts longer?
Implants generally outlast bridges. Studies show average bridge lifespan of 10–15 years, while implants with proper maintenance can last 20+ years. The implant crown may need replacement before the implant itself.
What about Maryland (adhesive) bridges?
Maryland bridges bond to the back of adjacent teeth without full crowning — less invasive than a traditional bridge. They work well for front teeth in certain situations, but they don't match the longevity or stability of an implant and can debond over time.
